2 reported dead due to typhoon Uwan as of Monday: NDRRMC

By Jauhn Etienne Villaruel, Michael Delizo | November 10, 2025

MANILA (UPDATED) — The National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) on Monday said 2 individuals were reported dead due to typhoon Uwan. In an 11 a.m. media briefing, Office of Civil Defense Officer-in-Charge Raffy Alejandro IV said one victim drowned in Viga, Catanduanes, while another died in Catbalogan City, Samar after a structure collapsed. Of these, 426,000 families or 1.4 million individuals were pre-emptively evacuated, with 92,000 families or 318,000 individuals staying in evacuation centers

As of Monday morning, the NDRRMC said a total of 1,085 damaged houses were reported in Western Visayas, Zamboanga Peninsula, and CARAGA. At least 155 cities and municipalities are still experiencing power interruptions, 13 have water interruptions, and 14 are dealing with communication issues. A total of 71 roads and 26 bridges remain impassable. Some municipalities in Aurora province — including Casiguran, Lasag, Dinalungan and Dipacilao — were reported isolated due to landslides. Clearing operations are ongoing in areas hit by fallen trees and soil collapse. Flooding persists in 132 areas, while 13 communities reported storm surges. The weather disturbance also triggered the suspension of 995 classes.

Meanwhile, four barges ran aground at the height of strong waves in San Jose, Antique; Nasugbu, Batangas; and San Vicente, Palawan while docked at ports. No casualties were reported and there is no expected oil spill. In its 11:00 a.m. advisory, state weather bureau PAGASA said Uwan was located 135 km west northwest of Bacnotan, La Union, packing winds of 130 kph and gusts up to 160 kph. It was moving west northwestward at 20 kph. Wind signal no. 3 was still hoisted over Ilocos Sur, northern and central La Union, and northwestern Pangasinan. Prior to the briefing, Alejandro said the NDRRMC has updated President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and Defense Secretary Gilbert Teodoro on the latest situation. The President commended local government units and agencies for preparedness, mitigation efforts, and pre-emptive evacuations that helped minimize casualties and damage. He also ordered continuous response and relief operations. Alejandro added that Typhoon Uwan has exited the Philippine landmass, but scattered rains continue to affect parts of Luzon.
